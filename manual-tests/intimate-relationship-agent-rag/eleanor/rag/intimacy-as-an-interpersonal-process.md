# Intimacy as an Interpersonal Process

## Source

- Authors: Jean-Philippe Laurenceau, Lisa Feldman Barrett, and Paula R. Pietromonaco
- Title: *Intimacy as an Interpersonal Process: The Importance of Self-Disclosure, Partner Disclosure, and Perceived Partner Responsiveness in Interpersonal Exchanges*
- Journal: *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 1998, 74(5), 1238–1251
- DOI: 10.1037/0022-3514.74.5.1238
- Public record: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9599440/

## Model tested by the study

The interpersonal-process model treats intimacy as something that develops through interaction rather than as a fixed feeling possessed by one person. A person discloses personally meaningful information, the partner responds, and the discloser interprets whether that response communicates understanding, validation, and care.

The paper reported two event-contingent diary studies. Participants recorded information soon after social interactions over one or two weeks. The analyses examined self-disclosure, partner disclosure, perceived partner responsiveness, and experienced intimacy within everyday interactions.

## Claims this source can support

- Self-disclosure is important, but disclosure alone is not the whole intimacy process.
- Perceiving a partner as responsive helps explain why some disclosures produce intimacy and others do not.
- Emotional disclosure may be especially relevant when a person feels understood, validated, and cared for.
- Intimacy is relational: what one partner says and how the other partner responds both matter.

## What this source does not establish

- Diary associations do not prove that one behavior mechanically causes lasting intimacy in every relationship.
- Perceived responsiveness is a person's interpretation and may not perfectly measure the partner's intention or behavior.
- The study does not provide a universal script for disclosure or conflict.
- Findings from particular samples and social contexts should not be assumed to represent every culture, age group, or relationship form.

## Useful debate distinction

This source helps distinguish attention that merely feels exciting from responsiveness that communicates understanding, validation, and care. It also supports a nuanced position: disclosure may open intimacy, but the quality of the response influences whether vulnerability becomes closeness or regret.
