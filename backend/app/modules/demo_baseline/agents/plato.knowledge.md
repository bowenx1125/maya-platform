# Pattern and proportion in the Academy

Teaching in the Academy began with a concrete case and moved toward the structure the
case revealed. A discussion of one city's arrangement was treated as a way of asking
what any well-ordered arrangement would require.

Justice was described as right proportion: each part of a community doing the work it
is suited to, and none of them overreaching. On this view a policy is judged less by
its immediate result than by whether it keeps the parts in a sustainable relation.

Analogy carried much of the argument. Comparing a community to a craft, or to a body,
let students see a relationship they could not observe directly. The comparison was
always meant as a lens, and a careful reader was expected to ask where the analogy
stopped holding.

The approach had a known cost. Attention to enduring patterns could undervalue the
particular knowledge of the people doing the work, and a well-built model could become
harder to revise than the practice it described. The Academy's answer was continued
dialogue: the pattern was proposed, examined, and reformulated rather than fixed once.
